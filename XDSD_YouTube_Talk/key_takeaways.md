# Key Takeaways

**Video:** eXtremely Distributed Software Development (XDSD), at DevTernity'2016
**Link:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7EytYc7K5JA

## 🎯 Key Takeaways for quick navigation

- **00:00** 🤖 Traditional software development often faces chaos, missed deadlines, and customer dissatisfaction.
- **01:27** 🧠 Software project failures are primarily due to management issues, not technical incompetence.
- **02:23** 💼 XDSD methodology focuses on individual responsibility over team dynamics.
- **05:46** 🕒 XDSD discourages overtime, emphasizing voluntary work and results-based compensation.
- **08:41** 📚 XDSD promotes knowledge sharing through documentation and discourages reliance on experts.
- **19:50** 📈 In the XDSD model, tasks are paid only when they are successfully closed, making it easy to measure performance and motivation.
- **20:19** 💼 XDSD has been used by 250 developers across 25 projects, mostly in Java, resulting in successful outcomes and extensive Java code.
- **22:16** 📐 In XDSD, the final design and architecture decisions are made by one person, often the architect, with input from others.
- **23:13** 🗣️ Communication in XDSD happens through tickets, not meetings, providing clear traceability and transparency.
- **34:14** 💰 XDSD pays based on results, allowing developers to manage their time and finances independently.
- **37:36** 💡 In eXtremely Distributed Software Development (XDSD), estimates are avoided, and costs are not fixed upfront. Instead, the team adapts to changes as they arise, focusing on not cheating the client.
- **38:04** 💬 Developers forming secret chat groups can undermine XDSD principles. Clear communication through tickets is essential to maintain transparency and control.
- **40:28** 💼 Bugs introduced intentionally by developers are assigned randomly for fixing. This discourages developers from generating bugs for extra income.
- **41:54** 💰 Payment for tasks in XDSD is based on a standard hourly rate for developers. The budget is determined by multiplying the hourly rate by the estimated effort, which is always stated as a fixed amount of time (e.g., one hour).
- **44:18** 🛠️ XDSD hasn't yet incorporated DevOps practices extensively, but it may evolve with the advent of immutable server infrastructure. Currently, server-related issues are also addressed using XDSD principles, with budgets adjusted for urgency.
